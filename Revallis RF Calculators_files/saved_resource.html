The damper is the biggest lever and the most dangerous one to pull. Raising it sends more power to the loop, so less is needed for the same field. It also raises Q, which narrows the match and hands repeatability back to every unit's individual loss. That trade is the reason R105 exists at all. Tank voltage does not move with it: it is set by the field and the loop, not by the damper.
Validated against loop-inductance.md §8b: Litz at R105 = 7.5 kΩ for 5 µT reproduces 414 mW into the loop, 524 mW into the damper and Q = 36 to the milliwatt. Passive side only, at resonance, and it assumes the network presents the feed impedance at TP103. It does not model C110/C111 individually, the class-E side, or harmonics.

The stage and the tank disagree by 113%. At 17.8 V into 50 Ω an ideally switching stage delivers 2.879 W, while the tank needs 1.350 W. Either the rail is higher than this load needs, or the stage is not converting at the efficiency the equations assume. Measuring DC input during a burst separates those two, and nothing else does.
Gate demand exceeds the driver. 33 mA against a 24 mA rating. That rating is a static figure rather than a peak limit, so the gate is still driven, but the output sags below its guaranteed levels while charging and edges slow. Class E assumes fast switching.

Sokal's expressions have a pole at Q_L = 1.7879, visible in C2's denominator, and they are stated as valid above it. At Q_L = 2.8 that term contributes 1.00; at 2.0 it is 4.78 and at 1.9 it is 9.05, so the equations go stiff well before they go singular. Treat anything under about 2.2 as needing a different method rather than a smaller number.
Q_L is the one genuinely free parameter. Raising it shrinks C1, which pushes C119 down toward C_oss and hands the tank back to the transistor. Lowering it grows C1 and C2 but weakens harmonic filtering, which the board has no low-pass stage to make up for. 2.8 was chosen deliberately, not inherited.
Efficiency is measured, not assumed. 74% is what reconciles this stage with the Beehive probe on 2026-09-05: at 17.8 V into 50 Ω the equations offer 2.879 W, and the field the probe reports needs 2.132 W of it. It is a single-point calibration at one operating point, so treat it as an anchor rather than a law. Class E should reach 85 to 95%, and the shortfall is the open question in class-e-design.md §9. Set it to 100 to see what a perfectly switching stage would give.
Sokal's finite-Q equations (QEX 2001). Validated against classe2 at the design point: C1 and L2 exact, R within 0.5%. C2 differs by about 1.2% because classe2 omits the choke correction term for C2 while applying it to C1; the difference is well inside C107's trimmer range. Peak-voltage factor is fitted through the three values in class-e-design.md §4 and reproduces all three exactly. Ideal switching is assumed throughout: no R_DS(on) loss, no transition loss, so the power figure is what a perfectly switching stage would deliver, not what this one does.
