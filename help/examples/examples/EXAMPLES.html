QuickSmith

Worked examples

Solved examples from various text books, meant to introduce the tool rather than to teach the theory. They stay close to the application and do not go far into the mathematics, so keep a standard text on transmission lines and the Smith chart beside you for the basics.

Start here

Resistance and reactance on a Smith chart

What a series resistor, a series reactance and a shunt reactance each do to the input impedance. Three traces: along the real axis, round a constant resistance circle, and round a constant conductance circle.

Designing an L-network: 500 ohms to 50 ohms

The most common matching network there is, worked through at 100 MHz. A shunt capacitor to reach the 50 ohm circle, then a series inductor to tune out what is left.

Matching with a transmission line and an open stub

The same idea with distributed parts instead of lumped ones. A 30 ohm series line and a 30 ohm open stub take 10 - j15 ohms to 50 ohms at 1 GHz.

Going further

A wideband match for a dipole antenna

Matching across a band rather than at one frequency, with a load whose impedance changes as you sweep. Measured dipole data from 100 to 200 MHz, brought inside a 2:1 VSWR circle.

Insertion loss of a five pole Chebyshev filter

A filter rather than a match, and the example where component Q starts to matter. Real Q values on every part, and what they cost in the passband.

Amplifiers

Large signal amplifier design: the AN721 input match

From Motorola application note AN721. Matching the 1.94 + j1.1 ohm input of a 2N5642 power transistor to 50 ohms at 175 MHz, to a maximum loaded Q of 10. The output match follows.

Small signal amplifier design with S-parameters

Stability, gain and noise on two Smith charts at once. A 6 GHz amplifier for the lowest noise figure at 10.55 dB available gain, then the networks that get you there.

A note on the numbers

Every figure quoted in these examples is checked against the solver on each test run, so the prose here and what the program computes cannot drift apart. Where they already disagreed, the test suite pins what QuickSmith actually calculates and says so: Example 5 asks for 60 dB at 100 MHz and the filter as published gives 53.25 dB.

Several of these open as a live circuit. Look for Open this circuit in QuickSmith at the foot of the page, or try Help → Guided examples in the program, which rebuilds them a component at a time and says what each one does.
